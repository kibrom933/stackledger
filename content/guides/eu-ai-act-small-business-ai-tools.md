Most freelancers and small teams are not building AI. They are **using** it: a writing assistant, a meeting notetaker, an AI voice for a video. The EU AI Act calls you a **deployer** when you use an AI system at work. This guide sorts the dates that matter for deployers from the ones aimed at the companies that build AI models.

*This is general information, not legal advice. If your use of AI touches hiring, credit, health or other sensitive decisions, talk to a lawyer.*

## The short answer

For a typical small team using off-the-shelf AI tools, three things matter:

1. **AI literacy** has applied since **2 February 2025**, in a softened form since the 2026 AI Omnibus.
2. **Transparency rules** in Article 50 became generally applicable on **2 August 2026**, with one part moved to **2 December 2026**.
3. **High-risk** rules were pushed back to **2 December 2027** for the systems listed in Annex III, which includes areas like recruitment, and to **2 August 2028** for AI built into regulated products.

## What the AI Omnibus changed

The AI Act's timetable changed in 2026. The European Commission proposed the "AI Omnibus" in November 2025, Parliament adopted it on 16 June 2026, the Council on 29 June 2026, and it was published in the Official Journal on **24 July 2026**.

That matters because many guides written earlier in 2026 still show the original dates. If a source says all high-risk rules apply from 2 August 2026, it is out of date.

## Timeline for deployers

| Date | What applies | Does it affect a small team using AI tools? |
|---|---|---|
| 2 February 2025 | Prohibited AI practices; AI literacy | Yes: do not use AI for banned practices, and support your team's AI literacy |
| 2 August 2025 | Rules for general-purpose AI models; governance; penalties framework | Mostly the model providers, not you |
| 2 August 2026 | The Act becomes generally applicable, including Article 50 transparency | Yes, if you publish or use AI-generated content in ways Article 50 covers |
| 2 December 2026 | Article 50(2), postponed by the Omnibus | Mainly providers of systems that generate synthetic content |
| 2 December 2027 | High-risk rules for Annex III systems (postponed from 2 August 2026) | Only if you use AI for high-risk purposes such as hiring decisions |
| 2 August 2028 | High-risk rules for AI in products under Annex I (postponed from 2 August 2027) | Rarely, for a small services business |

## AI literacy: softer, but still there

The original text required providers and deployers **to ensure** a sufficient level of AI literacy among the people who use AI on their behalf. The Omnibus changed this to **support the development of** AI literacy, and added that the obligation does not require anyone to guarantee a specific level of AI literacy for any individual. The Commission and member states are also given a role in publishing guidance and practical examples.

In practice, for a small team, that points to proportionate steps rather than a certification programme. How regulators will apply it remains to be seen.

## Transparency: labelling AI-generated content

Article 50 covers transparency, for example labelling AI-generated content. It became generally applicable on 2 August 2026, while the Omnibus moved Article 50(2), which deals with systems that generate synthetic content, to 2 December 2026. Codes of practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content were in draft during 2026.

If you publish AI-generated images, audio or video, for instance with an AI voice or avatar tool, check what the final code of practice and your tool's own labelling features say before you publish.

## What to do this quarter

- [ ] List the AI tools your team uses and what you use each one for.
- [ ] Check that nothing falls under the **high-risk** uses, such as screening job applicants. If it does, get legal advice now; you have until December 2027, not forever.
- [ ] Give everyone who uses AI tools a short, practical briefing: what the tools are allowed to see, how to check outputs, and when to label AI content.
- [ ] Decide how you will **label AI-generated media** you publish.
- [ ] Read each tool's terms on **data retention and model training**. That is a GDPR question as much as an AI Act one, and we cover it in every review.
